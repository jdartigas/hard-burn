How to play

Ships take real damage: a hit that gets through to the hull can knock out a weapon, the engines, the shield generator, point defense, the sensors (worse aim, and no long-range railgun shots while they're offline) or, on missile ships, the magazine (lost salvos, and it can cook off). Damaged systems work worse; offline ones are out for a couple of turns. Crews patch things up over time, faster near a Repair tender, and its Resupply fixes everything.

Each turn every one of your ships can move up to its movement allowance, fire each of its ready weapons once, and use its special ability if charged. Move and fire in any order. When you end your turn, the enemy does the same. Destroy every enemy ship to win. If neither fleet is destroyed when the turn limit runs out (30 turns, 15 in a Quick battle), the side with more fleet value left holds the field.

Giving orders

  • Click one of your ships to select it. Highlighted hexes show where it can move; click one to burn there.
  • Hover an enemy to see your odds. Click it to fire every ready weapon that can reach, or pick a single weapon first.
  • Shields absorb damage first and recharge each turn, but only half as much after a turn under fire (three quarters for cruisers, carriers and dreadnoughts). Armor then blunts every hit to the hull.
  • A ship that blows up damages every ship in the hexes next to it, yours included. Don't finish off an enemy your own ship is parked beside, and keep clear of your wrecks-to-be.

Terrain

Large asteroids block movement and line of sight for direct-fire weapons, but they can be shot apart. Click one with a ship selected to fire on it; a shattered asteroid leaves a debris field behind. Debris fields cost double movement but give ships inside them cover. Destroyed ships break apart and leave a wreck field of the same kind. Frigates screen allies within 3 hexes with their full point defense: watch for the tracer fire from the escort when it knocks down a missile.
